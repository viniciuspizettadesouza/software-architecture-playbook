# Playbook Methodology

This playbook combines lessons from real projects with established software engineering literature.

Recommendations are contextual, not universal. The repository is a practical memory aid, not an evidence-ranking or academic approval system.

When adding knowledge:

1. Identify the concept or decision in plain language.
2. Explain what it means and why it matters.
3. Describe when it is useful.
4. Record trade-offs and ways it can be overused.
5. Connect it to related concepts.
6. Add an example from a project when one helps understanding.
7. Link literature or standards that provide useful depth.

A concept page should normally answer: what is it, why does it matter, when is it useful, what can go wrong, and what is related?

Project pages are examples, not proof. Preserve the interesting decisions and lessons without scores, confidence levels, snapshot governance or promotion rules.

Technology pages should use practical labels: **Default**, **Use when needed**, **Alternative** or **Avoid by default**.

Keep the structure proportional to its purpose. A short, useful note is better than an empty formal template.
